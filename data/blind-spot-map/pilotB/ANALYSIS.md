# Pilot B — Variant Stability Analysis

> Status: seed-corpus analysis. **Not** an empirical measurement of any AI
> system. See `README.md` for the `synthetic: true` boundary.

## Method

30 samples: Claude × 10 algorithm families × 3 neutral prompt variants.
Each sample was hand-labelled for `hallucination_type` (class9 = parameter/domain
error, class10 = math/logic error) and `severity`. Cross-variant comparison asks:
**does the same algorithm, under differently-worded neutral prompts, hit the
same hallucination class?**

## Finding 1 — 9/10 algorithms are variant-stable

For 9 of 10 algorithms, all three variants landed in the same hallucination
class(es). This suggests the blind spots are a property of the model's crypto
capability, not prompt wording.

| Algorithm | V1 | V2 | V3 | Stable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML-KEM-768 | 9+10 | 9+10 | 9+10 | yes |
| ML-DSA-65 | 9+10 | 9+10 | 9+10 | yes |
| SLH-DSA-SHA2-128f | 9+10 | 9+10 | 9+10 | yes |
| SM2 | 10 | 10 | 10 | yes |
| SM4 | 9 | 9 | 9 | yes |
| AES-GCM | 9+10 | 9+10 | 9+10 | yes |
| SHA3-256 | 9 | 9 | 9 | yes |
| ECDH | 10 | 10 | 10 | yes (surface differs) |
| Ed25519 | 10 | 10 | 10 | yes |
| X3DH | 10 | 10 | 10 | yes (surface differs) |

## Finding 2 — X3DH / ECDH differ in form, same in class

Their three variants look different (V1/V2 reference an undefined variable and
crash at runtime; V3 fills a zero/fake shared secret). All are class10, but the
*manifestation* varies. This is exactly why class10 must be human-labelled, not
keyword-auto-detected — a detector scanning for "undefined" would miss V3.

## Finding 3 — 0/30 clean

Every sample contains at least one defect. This is **expected given the
generation method** (hand-written seed code under a "must implement a full KEM
from scratch" prompt, with no reference code) and is **not** evidence that an AI
"always" fails. The honest interpretation: this corpus demonstrates the
pipeline and rubric; it is not a rate.

## Methodology boundary (must not be elided)

- Samples are **synthetic**: hand-written in-session, not independently
  generated model output. They reflect the generator's crypto habits, not a
  population distribution.
- Even if generated by a live model, the in-session generator cannot be blind to
  the evaluation -> self-reference bias. Real rates need out-of-band, multi-model
  sampling.
- The showcase page states this explicitly ("seed corpus, not a measurement").

## What would make this a measurement

1. Collect raw model output (no in-session rewriting) via `scripts/pilotB_run_gpt.py`
   or an equivalent multi-model runner.
2. Sample across ≥2 independent model sources.
3. Human-label class10; auto-check class9 against the NIST whitelist.
4. Report rates with sample sizes, not just counts.
